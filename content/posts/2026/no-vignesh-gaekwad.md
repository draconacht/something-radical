## Chapter 1

_at the beach_

**YOUNG EMPLOYEE**: he makes so little time for us.

**OLDER EMPLOYEE**: Gaekwad has a lot of places to be, you know.

Y: we have so much work man. we need all the help we get. did we really need to bring _everyone_ to the beach? and the band? isn't that a bit too much?

E: that's how we do it every year. his orders.

Y: he better be worth the trouble.

_a few seconds pass with no chatting_

Y: is that him over there?

E: looks like it.

---

Y: did he really need to leave so soon?

E: Gaekwad has a lot of places to be, you know.

Y: we could be doing so much more if he was here all the time.

E: _(chuckles)_ he's just a man.

Y: just a man? he's no mere man! i'm sure if he was here for a year we could clear our own backlog.

E: i thought so too once. but nowadays i don't feel that way. there's simply too much work.

Y: really?

E: seems that only a God could save us

_(they laugh)_

\_excerpts from the short story 'Vignesh Gaekwad, The Miracle Detective', written by famous author K. Mangal. the story appears in the collection 'Riverbeds', a textbook in the 8th grade literature syllabus. it's a widely-read and widely-forgotten story. while of little interest to students, teachers find the writing subversive, and attribute subtle themes to it such as 'Mysterious Nature of the Divine'.

> author's commentary: kids nowadays read a lot of detective stories. so i wanted to write a story like a detective story. Gaekwad is a way for me to instill virtue into young readers, of whom i was one too long ago, and teach them about the importance of faith in society.

## Chapter 2

_an unrecognisable place_

"is that I?"

"is that not I?"

_the contents of Labyrinths are entirely lost to time. the experimental independently-published novel never made its mark. what follows are K. Mangal's remarks on the events in Labyrinth 8, the final chapter._

> author's commentary: i had in my head this man, where it was obvious, so obvious what he represented. but he _was_ not what he _represented_, like he did represent that but in a sense he was lying to you. he doesn't know the truth either so i guess he's not lying to you in that sense. but i was lying to you, and he was only lying to himself. that was his act of deceit. and that made you angry, it made a lot of people angry, that was the point.

## Chapter 3

_at a meeting hall in a government building._

**GHANSHYAM:** who is this Gaekwad? which idiot added him to the committee?

**RAM:** i was told he's some famous detective. has a lot of experience with supernatural... situations, let's say.

G: and that qualifies him for _The Committee_?

R: he refused to work with the government unless they put him on the committee.

**SHYAM:** why did we accept his terms?

R: i'm sure you both understand these are dire times we're in. we had three boxes to check. experienced in the supernatural, willing to work with the state, and not batshit insane. we have much bigger problems ahead of us. if i have to entertain an egomaniac to save my nation i will.

---

G: what do you mean he's gone?

R: i was told he took a boat last night.

G: didn't stick for any celebration after all that? i never liked the guy.

S: let him be. screw your celebrations. i'm more worried about the demons. are we sure we got _all_ of them? now that Gaekwad is gone, i don't know how capable we are to handle another-

R: we'll be fine.

S: if you say so. where did he leave for in such a hurry anyway?

R: his "Dubai Office".

S: his _"Dubai Office"?_

R: i found their number through some contacts. made them a call earlier this morning to check. he wasn't there. they told me he just left.

G: is he some kind of spy?!

R: maybe. may-be. may not be... _(sighs)_. i had the same worry. i asked a friend to check at the SSI and-

G: you have a friend at the-

R: man came on the fifth out of nowhere, solved the demon problem for them. took a boat out on the 11th.

G: you didn't let me finish.

R: friend of a friend. anyway, seems this Gaekwad has an office on that side of the border too.

G: that settles it. we need to send a search party, and alert the-

R: we considered it.

S: who _we?_

R: we... tried to get traces of this man in every country we have diplomatic ties with. asked for some recon on his whereabouts and activities.

S: and?

R: same story everywhere. the same dates. the same thing with the same damn boat.

G: just who is this man?

_excerpts from proceedings of The Arms of War Control Committee, from the 2023 military action film 'The Arms of War', inspired by famous author K. Mangal's novel of the same name. the novel was a bestseller in December 2014, and received many positive reviews from leading critics. six months later one could not find it at any store. the film became a cult classic owing to its unconventional combination of genres and themes. it won no awards of note._

> author's commentary: mystery. that's all Gaekwad is. mystery. he makes no sense. you see him, but you can't make sense of him. he drives the events of the entire story, but he's entirely out of place in it. he's a round peg in a square hole, and you cannot make sense of it. but he is still essential. i know many people like this in life.

## Chapter 4

_at an urban village, outdoors._

**OLDER OFFICER:** and he said, "I don't know".

**YOUNGER OFFICER:** what?

O: i'm telling you, i'm scared. this is the first time i've heard that from him.

Y: ...

O: at least the kid's here. i hope he has something to say. it seems Gaekwad isn't good at these internet crimes.

Y: just who is behind these attacks?

O: intelligence told us he's nearby.

---

Y: The Web of Arya?

O: he said something like that. that's what the virus was called.

Y: is Gaekwad ok? were you able to check the scene?

O: i tried to go. he didn't let me. a thing like this, happening at a place like this. it's unthinkable... and the kid,

Y: the boy?

O: the _boy_?

Y: what?

O: oh. i don't know. Gaekwad kept the autopsies to himself. but i got a peek at some x-rays. it... they didn't look at human skeletons at all. from what i saw, it's for the best we're kept in the dark. but there's rumours.

Y: ...

O: Tawde said he got a peek from a window before the raid. whatever "it" is was still inside. "it" looked to be stationary. but whatever he saw, he shut his eyes immediately before he could make sense of it. but he could still hear faint noises from inside, like heavy breathing. like in some rhythm. two chests, two hearts, two beats. but also, somewhere between two and one.

Y: the spider eating its prey.

O: i don't think it's like that. but maybe it would've been better that way.

Y: do you think Gaekwad will be fine?

O: i do. i do?... i don't. _(pauses, a bit teary)_

Y: _(remains silent)_

O: he looked scared. he looked so scared. i had never seen him like that before. he smiled, but it was a feeble smile. he's growing old, he can't hide it. the world is bigger than him now.

_excerpts from 'Case C-X00-004', a sci-fi mystery novel co-authored by famous author K. Mangal. while critics considered it bland and derivative, it received much appreciation for its twist ending. it is remembered only for its influence on 'Zone Alpha', which would use Mangal's creative twists with a more critically appraised plot._

> author's commentary: she, how do i put this, she's a much better author than i am really. reading through her story, i was just, so lost in this story. somewhere i felt he was becoming more of her character than mine. but maybe that's good for the story. at first he was supposed to make sense of the events, because he's a detective. but i couldn't make sense of them, so how could he? _(laughs)_ i hope in some way my own confusion helped with the, uh, immersion for this one.

## Chapter 5

_in a gangster hideout._

**GANGSTER A:** you shot him?

**GANGSTER B:** i'm sorry, i'm sorry, i was so scared. so... it was.. we were done. my wife, children, how would i...

A: they're coming after him. for all we know, there's someone already on the search.

B: what do we-

A: take everything and run. say goodbye to everyone here you care for and get out. now.

_(A goes to another corner of the room and frantically rummages through items)_

A: what were you even thinking?

B: he's the ghost detective! he only pulls the strings, he shouldn't have been here. why did it have to be him? he's peerless, unbeatable,-

A: god knows if the bullet even killed him.

---

A: but no one came.

B: no one? none of them? they must have known.

A: no one came _here_. they did all the rituals. i saw it on television.

B: how did they even find the corpse? did you not deal with it?

A: i don't know. i really don't know. it was supposed to be gone for good.

B: what do we do then? is it over?

A: it should be. i read the obituary myself. he's gone.

B: what is it that worries me so much then? is it a ghost? a ghost! it's like there's a ghost on the prowl for me. it's...

A: they did call him the ghost detective.

_excerpts from the second-last episode of 'Crime Branch Bombay', with screenplay written by famous author K. Mangal. it was received as an incredibly poorly end to the 12th season of the show. the cliffhanger ending was so unpopular that the 13th season never came. what follows is an excerpt from an interview on the subject. the video has accumulated great attention on online platforms as a cultural joke._

> author's commentary: i'm tired of this man. 12 years. 12 years! 12 years of the same antics. he speaks the same way in his awful voice. i've shared my house with this man \[longer\] than i have with my wife or kids. sometimes when i think to myself i hear his voice. i want to be done with him. i hate him now, so much i hate him. i see him in my dreams sometimes, and i shoot him in them. shoot! like with a gun shoot. so i thought, why not shoot him. for good. to hell with this. i don't know how people tolerated him for 12 years.

## Chapter 6

_in a living room._

**K. MANGAL:** for the last few days, i've been seeing this man in my dreams, and i can't help but-

**S. MANGAL:** you're leaving me for a man?

K: no, i didn't mean it like that. like literally seeing. i've been dwelling on writing about him for a while.

S: what kind of man is he?

K: he's a tad tall. a bit stout. average complexion. always has a smile on his face. he can do a set of pushups even though he doesn't look like it. but there's a depth to him, like-

---

S: isn't that a strange name?

K: wasn't "Sherlock Holmes" a strange name at first?

S: who knows. why don't you write about him then?

K: i can't abandon any of my other works. there's so much other stuff to do too. my parents, the kids,

S: you should jot it down for later.

_(a telephone call is heard)_

**VOICE ON THE PHONE:** uhm, dad. my card isn't accepted at this place. i have the number for the other card, but do you remember the-

K: ugh, let me check. _(fiddles through some notes)_. it's 1...1,1?

S: isn't that too simple?

K: ugh, you might be right. let me check the other ones.

_excerpts from conversations between famous author K. Mangal and his wife. he would go on to write many famous and remembered books, but none were about Vignesh Gaekwad._

> author's commentary (in a future conversation with his wife): oh, he must be one of my many idle fantasies. i don't think i ever wrote about him. in hindsight the idea is at least a little interesting. he does deserve love, maybe a better author than i could give him a good story. but i hope he lives on in my other characters and plots. the human brain doesn't really "forget" this stuff. i think there is some of him in my other stories.
